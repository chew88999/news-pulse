# Top Three News: product brief
**In one line:** Shows IT and engineering staff the three news items that matter to them, with enough context to judge whether each is true.

## Problem
Related news reaches IT and engineering staff from too many places at once: internet sites, and colleagues and bosses on WhatsApp. In the moment an article arrives, you cannot tell fast enough whether it is factual or relevant, because WhatsApp groups are your main way of passing information and you have no way to check what is factual. The team has not yet landed on a cause it could change. If nothing changes, you stay overwhelmed and risk missing the few items that matter.

## Evidence
- You and your colleagues are the users; articles arrive from internet sites, colleagues and bosses.
- You cannot judge factuality and relevance fast enough; today it takes more than 3 hours.
- Not checked yet: whether staff want their news narrowed to three items rather than scanning everything themselves.

## Success
- **Metric:** Time to reach the three most important news items.
- **Today:** more than 3 hours
- **Target:** less than 15 minutes; date not set
- **Must not get worse:** not set

## Riskiest bet
That IT and engineering staff want their news narrowed to three items rather than scanning everything themselves.
- **Test:** You said there is no 30-minute test without code; the closest is checking each item against its source by hand.
- **Pass mark:** The three items come back with their sources and a note on whether each is factual, and you accept them as the three that matter.
- **Result:** not run yet

## First version
1. As an IT or engineering staff member, I open a page and see the three most important articles. Done when exactly three are listed.
2. As an IT or engineering staff member, I see each article's source and whether it is factual. Done when both show.
3. As an IT or engineering staff member, I get a short summary of each. Done when each has one to two lines.

## Walkthrough
1. You open the webpage.
2. You see the three most important articles.
3. Each carries a one to two line summary, its source, and an assessment of whether it is factual.
4. You decide which to act on.
If it goes wrong: the article is tagged with a warning icon saying "fake" or "to be verified", and you check the source yourself before acting.

## Not building
- More than three articles per view.
- Any way to receive or forward news inside WhatsApp.
- Beyond the webpage summary: no history or archive.

## Open questions
- What must not get worse, and by when should the 15 minutes be reached?
- Is narrowing to three items what staff actually want? Nothing has tested it.
- Step 2 never landed on a cause you could change. Which will you commit to?
